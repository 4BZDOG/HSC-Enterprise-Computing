## Year 11 — Interactive media and the user experience

Practical experiences and project work are an essential component of the Enterprise Computing Stage 6 course. Practical experiences provide opportunities for students to apply their knowledge and skills in authentic contexts. Project work develops student’s skills and capabilities in planning, designing and developing a digital solution. Practical experiences and project work should be accessible for and inclusive of all students. Flexible approaches to using a range of tools and providing opportunity for student choice help to ensure meaningful participation for every student. Students should also be encouraged to present their project work using a variety of modes, including reports, digital presentations, oral presentations, data visualisations or multimodal presentations.

Why is it important? This focus area provides an opportunity for students to identify interactive digital media and its applications. They explore the transfer of data in interactive digital media. Students investigate the ways in which digital media is used and how this influences the design and development of interactive media systems. Students are provided with opportunities to use design thinking skills in the design and development of interactive media systems for a specific user or function. They use systems thinking when they participate in a functional project that uses multimedia enterprises. Project work advice Teachers have the flexibility to plan projects with students that best address their individual needs and interests, as well as the context and resourcing of the school. Project work is an iterative process that builds on a systematic approach to problem identification, research, problem-solving, prototyping and evaluation. Project work also provides opportunities for students to learn about the value of planning and reviewing processes which are embedded in the design and production process. In this focus area, students are provided with opportunities to develop one or more projects that consider human-centred design. Projects explore the use of interactive media software and hardware to demonstrate the importance of digital design and interactive media as a communication tool. Projects may include a collection of media files, and the use of software to design and develop an interactive product. Potential projects include: presenting findings from the analysis of a website and its accessibility for the user (consider appearance, navigation, social and ethical issues) creating an interactive website showing viewers and listeners about a location of interest in your local area, using images, sound, voice and/or video to connect with the location.

Why is it important? In this focus area students investigate the effectiveness of a range of applications when communicating information, including the need to design for user experience (UX) and related social, ethical and legal issues. Students explore the capture, creation, transmission, storage, analysis, integration, presentation and sharing of interactive media files. They investigate hardware and software including storage, distributed computing, security protocols, the use of intellectual property and the importance of user interfaces (UI). Project work advice Students apply knowledge and skills to develop digital products that demonstrate the value of digital design and promote interactive media as a communication tool. Projects should demonstrate the safe and ethical use of online tools and use contemporary and innovative software. Projects may include the use of collected and/or curated media files to create a system for information delivery, a presentation using animations or an interactive learning system.

Considerations for teaching Life Skills outcomes and content: Specific Life Skills outcomes should be selected to meet the particular needs of individual students. Across a year or stage of schooling, students will work towards the number of Life Skills outcomes that are appropriate for their needs. Teachers can select the content from the focus areas that best addresses the selected outcomes. The focus areas provide a possible structure for addressing the Life Skills outcomes and content and are suggestions only. Life Skills outcomes cannot be taught in combination with other outcomes from the equivalent Stage 6 course. Students are required to demonstrate achievement of one or more of the Life Skills outcomes.

Further information in relation to planning, implementing and assessing Life Skills outcomes and content can be found on the NESA website.

Project work advice Teachers have the flexibility to plan projects with students that best address their individual needs and interests, as well as the context and resourcing of the school. They develop knowledge and understanding of digital citizenship when interacting online. Students develop skills in digital literacy and cyber safety, including keeping themselves safe and secure online. Students also develop appropriate communication and digital etiquette, including understanding the impact of their digital footprint. Project work advice Teachers have the flexibility to plan projects with students that best address their individual needs and interests, as well as the context and resourcing of the school. ## Year 12 — Data science

Why is it important? This focus area provides opportunities for students to develop an understanding of data and its uses. Students collect, record, store, use and report data. They explore social and ethical issues associated with storing, using and communicating data. Students explore how data can be used to effectively communicate key messages. They develop their understanding of the use of data in communication, transactions and online systems. Students consider security and safety issues associated with data systems and ways to protect small and large datasets. Students develop skills across a range of software applications as they collect, interpret and present data. They develop skills in the use of various applications to create meaningful data visualisations. Students explore the importance of data storage and communication and engage with data-system security in real-world scenarios. Project work advice Teachers have the flexibility to plan projects with students that best address their individual needs and interests, as well as the context and resourcing of the school. Project work is an iterative process that builds on a systematic approach to problem identification, research, problem-solving, prototyping and evaluation. Project work also provides opportunities for students to learn about the value of planning and reviewing processes which are embedded in the design and production process. In this topic, students apply core knowledge and skills to the development of one or more projects that collect, store, organise, analyse and present data. These projects explore the use of spreadsheets, databases and presentation software to communicate the analysis of data. Projects may include the safe and ethical use of online tools, collected and curated datasets including small and big data, and creating data visualisation and presentation methods. Potential projects include: producing and implementing a survey that collects qualitative and/or quantitative data and displays results graphically, then presenting these findings through a multimodal, visual, audio or written text providing a digital presentation to demonstrate factors affecting data collection, ownership and access for diverse cultural groups designing a method to collect data about dietary requirements and food preferences from 20 people invited to a dinner party. Using this information to design a menu. Finding recipes for the menu and using a spreadsheet to calculate the quantities needed for ingredients so that there is enough of each meal for the people at the dinner party.

Students assess the quality of data while analysing the types of data required to support the production of meaningful information. Students investigate social and ethical issues associated with collecting, securing, using and visualising data. ## Year 12 — Enterprise project

Why is it important? Students have the opportunity to develop deeper knowledge, understanding and skills in an area of interest as they engage in an enterprise project that addresses an identified real-world problem. Students refine project-management skills as they individually or collaboratively develop, document, implement and evaluate their project solution as outlined in the design and production process. Project work advice Teachers have the flexibility to plan projects with students that best address their individual needs and interests, as well as the context and resourcing of the school. Project work is an iterative process that builds on a systematic approach to problem identification, research, problem-solving, prototyping and evaluation. Project work also provides opportunities for students to learn about the value of planning and reviewing processes which are embedded in the design and production process. The process of design and production includes: identifying and defining researching and planning producing and implementing testing and evaluating. In this focus area, students identify a real-world problem of interest and develop a solution. Students follow the design and production process to explore factors that influence design and production and identify steps in the design process to produce a computing solution. Potential projects include: creating a program that communicates with other devices by sending and receiving messages wirelessly creating, executing and debugging a Python program building a prototype lighting system that produces light at different brightness levels depending on the value of the sensor readings creating a step-counter and using the device to monitor a diverse group of people over 7 days, then analyse the data.
